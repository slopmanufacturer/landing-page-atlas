02 / Collection

What we keep.

Manchester, mostly. The city in maps, minutes, photographs, and the things people wrote down when they thought nobody would read them.

The local history room holds 1,900 photographs, 310 zines, and every surviving street directory from 1817 to 1974. The map cabinet is the good furniture: twelve shallow drawers, linen-backed, with a laminated note asking you to wash your hands first.

We buy new fiction slowly, and we keep the odd copy. If a book is out of print and we have two, one goes in the reference bay. If we have one, it goes home with somebody. That is the whole acquisition policy, and it has outlasted three strategic reviews.

A library is a slow argument for the public good. We keep the receipts.

Ruth Ainsworth, librarian

04 / Join

A card, a number, a shelf.

Membership is free for anyone who lives, works, or studies in Greater Manchester. Bring proof of address the first time; after that, just your number. Children can join from birth, which is earlier than they can read.

No joining fee. No fines on children's books. Twenty pence a day on everything else, capped at the price of the book.

We reply within two working days. Your card is waiting at the desk.
