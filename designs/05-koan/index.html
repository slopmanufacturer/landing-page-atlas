The room

Four seats. One garden.
No menu after dark.

Kōan sits at the end of a narrow lane in Higashiyama, behind a gate that most people walk past. Inside there is a counter of old cedar, a window onto a garden the size of a tablecloth, and a kettle that has been in use since before we opened.

We do not take groups. We do not play music. The room is small on purpose, so that the weather can get in.

本日の茶

Spring is a lesson in restraint: the first pick, the first warmth, the first day you can leave the door open.

Tea is brewed to order.

間 Ma — the space between

Leave room.

The pause between two sounds is part of the music. We keep the room half empty so the tea has somewhere to go.

侘寂 Wabi-sabi — the beauty of wear

Let it age.

The counter is scratched. The garden is uneven. The cups do not match. None of this is an accident.

一期一会 Ichigo ichie — one meeting, once

Be here once.

Every bowl is different, even when it is the same tea. We will not be able to make this one again.
