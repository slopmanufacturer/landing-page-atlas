Warden Point
Est. 1862 / 41 m

A working lighthouse / Open to the public

One hundred
and sixty years
of not going out.

The tower is 41 metres of granite, the lamp is a fourth-order Fresnel lens, and the keepers are gone. The light still runs every night, and the door opens on Thursdays.

02 / The light

Every light
has a signature.

Mariners read a light by its rhythm before they read the chart. Warden Point flashes three times, then rests. Choose a character below to see how the tower would look with it.

Fl(3) W 15s — three flashes of one second, then eleven seconds of darkness. Visible for 18 nautical miles.

03 / History

Four dates
that matter.

The trust has the original keeper logs, the 1902 conversion drawings, and a cupboard of spare mantles.

  1. 1862

    First lit on 14 March, oil-fired, with a fixed white light and one keeper. The tower was built from local granite at a cost of £4,180.

  2. 1902

    Converted to paraffin and fitted with the fourth-order Fresnel lens that still turns today. The keepers' cottages were extended.

  3. 1987

    Automated. The last keeper, Ailsa Kemp, left in November after thirty-one years and a final log entry of two words: "Light lit."

  4. 2004

    The trust took the lease, reopened the tower, and put the light back on the tourist chart. It has run every night since.

04 / Support
